Organization

Tutors for a Cause

Since eighth grade, I have volunteered as a remote English tutor with Tutors for a Cause, teaching weekly video lessons to students at Phu Hai School in Huế, Vietnam. Over shared classroom monitors and unreliable connections, I've taught vocabulary and sentence structure to students who travel to a school computer lab just to get online — and who keep showing up anyway.

What began as an hour a week became a four-year relationship with the same students, and this past summer it became something more: I traveled beyond the screen to interview them about their lives, their obstacles, and their dreams — and wrote a feature article about what I found.

Curriculum expansion

After learning that my students practiced without English books at home, I began adapting our curriculum around what they actually have: handwritten notebooks, borrowed laptops, and determination. I've been developing lesson materials that work offline, pace conversations for confidence rather than speed, and let students respond in writing when speaking feels out of reach.

Leadership initiatives

As one of the organization's longest-serving tutors, I now help onboard new volunteers — sharing what four years of frozen screens and brave silences have taught me about teaching across languages. My current project is telling my students' stories: my feature "Beyond the Screen," photographed by Rocco Ho, is being pitched to national and diaspora outlets to raise awareness and support for the program.

Conference

Asian American Footsteps Conference

The Asian American Footsteps Conference brings together Asian and Asian American students from independent schools across New England for a day of speakers, workshops, and community. For a girl who is often "the only one from Vietnam" at her school, AAFC is the rare room where the quiet parts don't need explaining.

I've attended with my school's delegation, connecting with students who navigate the same space between cultures — and bringing what I learn back to our own campus community.

Event recap

From the opening keynote to the affinity workshops, the conference was a full day of stories that sounded like mine — told by students and speakers who had turned that in-between feeling into art, activism, and leadership. We left with new friends from a dozen schools and a group chat that's still active.
