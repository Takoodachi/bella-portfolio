Tutors for a Cause

Tutors for a Cause is an international education nonprofit that teaches English to underserved students in Vietnam, including at Phu Hai School in Huế. I joined as a remote tutor in eighth grade; today, as Co-President, I direct an international team of 15 tutors serving more than 200 Pre-K through 7th grade students. Over shared classroom monitors and unreliable connections, our students travel to a school computer lab just to get online — and keep showing up anyway.

This past summer I went beyond the screen, interviewing my students about the physical, economic, informational, and relational obstacles between them and an education — and wrote a feature article, with photojournalism, about what I found.

Curriculum expansion

Working with local educators, I diagnosed the systemic obstacles in our classrooms — unreliable technology, few books at home, and students too nervous to speak up. Then I designed and compiled an original suite of gamified lesson materials to make practice feel like play and keep students engaged between sessions.

Leadership initiatives

As Co-President, I've helped grow Tutors for a Cause from one eighth-grade volunteer into an international team of 15 tutors. I also tell our students' stories: my feature "Beyond the Screen," photographed by Rocco Ho, is being pitched to national and diaspora outlets to raise awareness and support for the program.

Asian American Footsteps Conference

As President of our school's Asian & Asian American Association, a 100+ member affinity group, I organized the regional Asian American Footsteps Conference for more than 200 students from over 20 schools — managing the programming, marketing, and logistics.

For a girl who is often "the only one from Vietnam" in the room, AAFC is the rare space where the quiet parts don't need explaining. I wanted every student who came to feel that too.

Event recap

The day was full of stories that sounded like mine, from students who had turned that in-between feeling into art, activism, and leadership. I ran a dumpling workshop myself, and it became the conference's first session to sell out.
